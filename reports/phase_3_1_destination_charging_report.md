## 1. What was done

- **What changed:**
  - The Phase 3 public AC / destination shape (EVI-Pro Lite public L2, a US simulation) was replaced by an **industry-informed** shape built from qualitative peak timings reported by one industry practitioner. Those timings are unverified, and no magnitudes were given.
  - Weekday: three smooth peaks at 12-14, 17-18 and 20-22 h. Weekend and dated holidays: one 12-16 h plateau.
- **What was held fixed:**
  - Annual public AC energy: 15% of BEV energy, 192.747 GWh at 20% xEV.
  - Residential, workplace and public DC series: bit-identical to Phase 3.
  - All Phase 3 outputs: regenerated byte-identical.
- **Coverage:** 3 adoption scenarios × 2 mileage cases × 5 charging cases, plus 36 magnitude/width/weekend variants, an occupancy reading, public AC shares of 10-25%, three location mixes and five ToU participation levels.

**Answers to the brief's questions:**
- **Three weekday peaks:** represented. Regions at 12-13, 17 and 20-21 h, each with ≥ 10% prominence, in every variant except two flagged wide-width variants (fig26, fig28).
- **Weekend plateau:** represented.
  - Unimodal, with 51.6% of daily energy in 12-16 h and every hour 12-15 ≥ 80% of the maximum (fig27, fig29).
  - At equal daily energy, the weekend hourly peak is 1.44× the weekday peak. This is interpretation 1 of the reported "markedly larger" weekend peak.
- **Combined BEV maximum:** slightly *lower* (−3.9 MW, −1.0%), on the same date and hour.
  - The reason is that the industry-informed shape has a trough at 18-19 h, between the after-work and dinner peaks. That is exactly when residential arrivals peak; the EVI-Pro shape is at its high there (7.4% vs 6.7% at 18 h).
  - Public AC contributes 39.6 MW at the 18:00 maximum, against 43.5 MW in the reference (fig31).
- **Energy in TNB peak:** higher, because the 17-18 and 20-22 peaks sit inside the 14:00-22:00 window.
- **The 22:00 rebound:** slightly *worse* (+15.6 MW), because the dinner peak tails into the 22:00 hour (4.8% vs 3.1%).

## 3. Overlap with residential charging

**Weekday evening (fig30, fig31):**
- Industry-informed public AC averages 51.3 MW over 20:00-22:00, against 22.5 MW in the reference. Residential averages 172.6 MW in the same hours.
- The weekday correlation between hourly public AC and residential load rises from 0.55 to 0.75.
- The dinner peak therefore *does* overlap residential charging, but at 20-21 h. That is after the 18:00 residential maximum, where total load is 300-340 MW rather than 372 MW.
- The after-work peak (17 h) overlaps the residential ramp-up. That is why after-work-dominant variants raise the maximum.

**Weekend (fig32):**
- The 12-16 h plateau does **not** create a new weekend BEV peak.
- Weekend total load at 12-16 h peaks at 277.9 MW (Sun 29 Dec 15:00), against 341.4 MW at 18:00 the same day.
- With m = 1.5 the midday maximum is 298.9 MW, still about 50 MW below the evening.
- The weekend maximum stays at 18:00 in every variant.

**The sign of the effect on the unmanaged maximum is not robust.** It depends on the assumed relative magnitudes, which the evidence does not give.

**Occupancy reading of "active usage"** (session-derived power, `occupancy_basis`):
- Power concentrates at the start of each window, so the after-work peak lands at 17:00 on the residential ramp.

- ToU 50%: weekday maximum 269.5 MW (+29.5); overall maximum 346.8 MW.
- Synchronized 100%: 985.9 MW.
- Public AC alone peaks at 108.0 MW.
- **This is the largest upward effect found.** It hinges on the undefined metric.

## 5. Effectiveness of residential ToU management

Only residential sessions are ToU-managed, so the **energy shifted** out of peak hours is identical: 187.2 GWh at 50% and 374.3 GWh at 100%.

| Participation | Weekday-max reduction from unmanaged (reference → Industry-informed) | Share of BEV energy in TNB peak (reference → Industry-informed) |
|---|---|---|
| 25% | 67.7 → 67.7 MW | 33.5% → 34.4% |
| 50% | 135.5 → 133.9 MW | 26.2% → 27.2% |
| 75% | 146.7 → 144.3 MW | 18.9% → 19.9% |
| 100% | 82.0 → 78.5 MW | 11.7% → 12.6% |

- Apparent effectiveness is essentially **unchanged**: about 1-4% less MW reduction, and about 1 percentage point more residual peak-period energy, because more unshiftable public AC energy sits in 17-22 h.
- The ToU working case (`managed_staggered_p50`) keeps its weekday maximum within 2.4 MW of Phase 3.
- The overall BEV maximum under ToU remains the **weekend evening** (residential, not managed).

## 7. Remaining evidence gaps

1. **What the "active usage" metric is.** Power, occupancy or sessions? This is the single largest driver of the result, spanning −13 to +22 MW on the unmanaged maximum.
2. **Relative magnitudes** of the weekday peaks, and weekend vs weekday **daily energy**.
3. **Coverage:** sites, operator(s), Peninsular share, sample size, year (pre- or post-July-2025 ToU) and charger power.
4. **The 2030 AC destination network:** connectors, power and utilisation. At the modelled peaks, 6,500-9,400 vehicles charge simultaneously. That exceeds the roughly 3,900 non-DC public chargers counted nationally in March 2026 (secondary source), for **both** the reference and the industry-informed shapes.
5. **Malaysian measured location shares:** the 15% public AC share itself is unmeasured.
6. **The GSO system demand profile:** whether 12-14 h, 17-18 h or 20-22 h BEV load matters depends on system demand at those hours.

## 8. Recommendation

**Option 2: keep the industry-informed profile as an additional sensitivity case. Do not replace the public AC reference profile.** Phase 3 remains the main reference scenario.

**Reasons:**
1. **Source quality.**
   - The observations are the only Malaysian, practitioner-reported timing evidence for destination charging, and they are relevant. Excluding them (option 3) would discard real information. In particular, the US reference likely misses the evening (20-22 h) destination activity.
   - But they are a single, unverified, qualitative account. They give no magnitudes and an undefined metric. That is not sufficient to overturn a documented reference shape (option 1).
2. **Modelled results.**
   - With the neutral default, the main conclusions of Phase 3 are unchanged:
     - unmanaged maximum within 1% and at the same date and hour;
     - ToU working-case weekday maximum within 2.4 MW;
     - identical energy shifted;
     - no new weekend peak.
   - The variants span both signs (−12.8 to +21.6 MW), so adopting the profile as the main case would add assumption-driven variation without a defensible central value.
3. **What to carry into LEAP.**
   - Use `leap_bev_hourly_load_2030_industry_sensitivity.csv` alongside the Phase 3 main case.
   - Report two adverse bounds: `after_work_dominant` (unmanaged +4 to +9 MW) and the occupancy reading (+21.6 MW at 17:00).
   - For the 22:00 rebound stress test, note the extra +16 to +24 MW.
   - Re-examine the choice once the GSO profile is integrated: the hours where public AC differs (12-14 h and 20-22 h) may matter differently for the system than for the BEV-only maximum.
4. **What would justify promotion to the main case.**
   - An anonymised hourly kWh profile by day type, or a definition of "active usage" with relative magnitudes, from the source or a Malaysian charge-point operator.
   - With that, the public AC reference could be replaced directly with measured data. See the follow-up questions in `industry_evidence_assessment.md`, section 6.
